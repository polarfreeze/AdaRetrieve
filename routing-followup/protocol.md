# BM25 reuse and a single routing revision

The frozen submission and the original source-defined budgets are the reference. All new files and observations belong to this experiment. Each fold uses the original 384 training queries in each of its two source collections. Each source validation split uses its first 48 queries for threshold calibration and its next 48 queries for live confirmation. The trained encoders, root ridge router, GBDT and utility coefficients are frozen.

Five timed policies share the indexes. Sequential and one-shot each run with and without query-local lexical reuse. The fifth policy starts with the reused one-shot route and may change its remaining path once, immediately after the first dense retrieval. No extra probe is forced. The hidden lexical tail only becomes visible when the route requests expansion. A source training microbenchmark checks identical rankings, measures one and two lexical requests and supplies updated lexical cost estimates. The reuse policies update only those two action costs, with identical estimates for all three policies. All end-to-end clocks include initial retrieval, scoring, routing and budget checks.

The revision model fits ridge regressions on the source training oracle for quality gains relative to the observed dense prefix. The alternatives share the executed prefix, and may include stopping. Thresholds in predicted utility units are 0, 0.01, 0.025, 0.05 or disabled. Each fold and budget selects the highest source-calibration profiled utility, preferring disabled on ties. Thresholds and input hashes are saved before live confirmation.

The exploratory decision to spend T4 time on all three held-out tests uses only live source confirmation. For revision, require at least 0.003 macro utility gain at the middle or loose budget versus reused one-shot, a positive gain in at least two of three folds at that level, a tight-budget macro loss no larger than 0.005 and at least one actual revision at the qualifying level. For lexical reuse, require at least 5% lower macro mean latency for either original policy at a budget with macro utility loss no larger than 0.005, and faster latency in at least two folds there. If either change qualifies, test all five frozen methods to show each component separately. These are screening rules, not significance tests.

Randomize the five methods and three budgets within each query with a fixed seed. Report NDCG@10, measured utility, mean and p95 latency, overrun rates, revision rates and paired uncertainty. Give equal weight to folds and to their two source domains. For source intervals resample each distinct query within its source collection and keep both appearances together. For target intervals resample paired queries within each held-out collection. Report the previous exposure to the target results.
